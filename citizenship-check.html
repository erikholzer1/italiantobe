Why It Matters

More than a passport.

Italian citizenship is not a document you file away. It is a standing right to a different life, available to you the moment your claim is recognized.

🇪🇺 Live anywhere in the EU

Settle in any of the 27 member states without a visa and without a sponsor. You move under EU free movement law instead of going through a country's immigration system.

💼 Work in 27 labor markets

No work permit in any member state, employed or self-employed, with the same treatment on pay and conditions as a national. Licensing and tax rules still apply. The question of whether you are allowed to work there does not.

🚀 Build a business across borders

Open a company or work for yourself in another member state without a permit. Expanding from Italy into Spain or Portugal becomes a question of tax and registration rather than immigration.

🎓 Study at citizen rates

EU citizens are generally treated as domestic students rather than international ones, which can change the cost of a degree substantially. You can live in the country while you study, and work without a permit.

👨‍👩‍👧 Your children inherit it

Recognition can carry citizenship to your children, giving them the United States and the EU from birth. Transmission rules have tightened in recent years, so we look at the rules that apply at the time rather than assume.

💍 Your family comes with you

When you move to another member state, EU law gives your spouse and close family the right to join you, live there and work. That holds even when they are not EU citizens themselves.

Common Questions

Things people ask before they start

The process raises a lot of questions. Here are the ones we hear most often.

How far back can my Italian ancestor be?

Under Italy's reformed citizenship law (Law 74/2025, effective May 2025), jure sanguinis is now limited to two generations: your parent or grandparent must be the Italian-born ancestor. Previously, there was no generational limit.

Do I have to give up my US citizenship?

No. The United States allows dual citizenship, and Italy actively grants it. You keep your US passport in full and gain an Italian one alongside it.

How long does the whole process take?

It varies widely, anywhere from several months to several years, depending on your consulate, your ancestral commune, and document availability. We help you set realistic expectations from the start.

Can I do this without moving to Italy?

Yes. The consulate route lets you apply through the Italian consulate in your home country. No move required, though an Italy visit can sometimes speed up the commune records step.

What if my lineage goes through a woman born before 1948?

This is known as a 1948 case. You can still claim citizenship, but it requires filing directly with an Italian court, not the standard consular route. It's a longer process, but it works, and it's exactly the kind of thing the check below identifies for you.

What if I don't qualify by descent under the new rules?

Alternative pathways exist, including residency-based naturalization and citizenship through marriage. Take the Pre-Eligibility Check and we'll tell you plainly which of them may apply to your situation.
