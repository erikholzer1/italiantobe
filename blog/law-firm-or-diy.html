Should I Hire a Law Firm for Italian Citizenship or Do It Myself?

By Erik Holzer May 13, 2026 10 min read Italian Citizenship · Strategy

I submitted my citizenship application just days before a law changed that would have made me ineligible. Days. That is how much timing matters in this process, and why who you have in your corner matters even more.

This is the question I get asked most, and the honest answer is that it depends on your case. Some cases genuinely belong in front of an Italian court and need an avvocato representing you. Others are primarily a documentation and scheduling exercise, where the hard part is knowing which office holds which record and what will get your file rejected.

Let me break this down the way I wish someone had broken it down for me.

Where the Work Actually Sits

Two very different kinds of difficulty get bundled together under "Italian citizenship." The first is legal: whether a court proceeding is required, how a change in the law applies to your line, whether a break in the chain can be argued. That is work for a qualified Italian lawyer, and where your case needs it, ItalianToBe brings one in rather than improvising around it.

The second is procedural, and it is where most people actually lose time: tracking down records across several countries, getting the right version of each certificate, apostilles attached by the correct authority, sworn translations that hold up, and a comune or consulate whose requirements are specific and not always written down. None of that is decided by legal argument. It is decided by knowing the road.

What I do is the second kind of work, with legal support behind it for the first. You are not choosing between a lawyer and going it alone. You are choosing how much of the procedural load you want to carry yourself.

You are more than just a client. I have walked this road and now I will be leading the path for you.

What Doing It Yourself Actually Looks Like

Let me be direct about what the DIY path involves, because a lot of people underestimate it.

This is not a one-stop-shop operation. You will visit multiple offices scattered across the city. These offices do not communicate with each other. It is entirely up to you to connect the dots between them. You need to know which documents are required at each step, whether your family's documents are valid, and whether they meet the specific data requirements of each office, especially the comune, which tends to be the most particular.

Beyond the paperwork, you are navigating language barriers, bureaucratic systems that have no real equivalent outside Italy, and a timeline that is not just about the 90-day visa window but also about specific deadlines tied to different stages of the process. Miss a window and you may have to start again.

The silo problem

Each office only knows their step. They will not tell you what comes next and they often will not know. Some will send you somewhere wrong. You need to already know the full sequence before you walk in the door.

The Hidden Cost of Going With a Law Firm

The obvious cost is the fee, which I have seen reach well into five figures, often for work a standard case doesn't require. For a transparent breakdown of what different levels of service actually cost, see How Much Does It Cost to Hire an Italian Citizenship Service? But the less obvious cost is that you may be paying for far more than your case actually requires.

Lawyers are busy. They have full caseloads, meetings, and other clients. They are almost certainly not going to travel with you to different offices to make sure things go smoothly on the ground. The experience of working with a law firm tends to be remote, formal, and slow. You submit documents, you wait, you get an update, you wait again.

My service is built for exactly this gap. To walk with you. To be present for you. To be the person who knows what comes next and makes sure each step actually happens.

How Long Does This Take? The Honest Answer

People do not know what to believe on timeline. And frankly, there is no clean answer. Some people spend years on this journey. Others, with the right knowledge, the right relationships, and the drive to push through, get it done in months. I did.

What stretches timelines is uncertainty. Not knowing if you are in the right place. Not knowing if a document is valid. Not knowing if you missed something three steps ago that is going to surface later. Every one of those question marks costs time. And in a process where laws can change and visa clocks are ticking, time is the one thing you cannot afford to waste.

My service works for two types of people. The first is the self-starter who wants to carry the weight themselves but needs the playbook, the roadmap, and someone they can call when they hit a wall. The second is the person who wants to hand this off entirely and have someone walk every step with them.
